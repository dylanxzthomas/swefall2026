# Parent feedback sheet

**Concept:** Paste a school message or family update. Homeline turns it into events, assigned tasks, and things to buy. You confirm the details, then work from one family plan.

## Functions to react to

- Turn school messages into dates and tasks — MVP; automatic extraction needs a model key.
- See everyone’s upcoming commitments — MVP, entered manually.
- Assign responsibility for pickups and errands — MVP; no notifications sent.
- Catch overlapping events and unassigned work — MVP.
- Keep one household shopping list — MVP.
- Review the week when you need to — MVP.
- Read school emails automatically — possible next step.
- Maintain recurring pickups and activities — possible next step.
- Share changes with another caregiver — possible next step.
- Send reminders only when action is needed — possible next step.
- Plan meals around available time and groceries — idea to test.
- Prepare a babysitter handoff with the evening’s instructions — idea to test.
- Track school forms, payments, and deadlines — idea to test.
- Organize a change of plans when a child is sick — idea to test.

## Ask parents

- Walk me through the last household detail that slipped. Where did the information arrive?
- Who noticed it, planned the response, and did the work?
- What do you use now? Show me how you handled one recent school message.
- Which three functions would you use this week? Which would you ignore?
- What information would you willingly share? What should always need your approval?
- Would pasting an update save time, or would automatic inbox access be essential?
- Try one real update. Did Homeline leave you with something usable? What did you have to fix?
- What would this replace? What do you already pay for that solves part of this problem?
- After a week of use: would you pay to keep it? What result would justify that price?

## Compare different households

- Working parents: competing work commitments and school schedules.
- Stay-at-home parents: the daily coordination load and unfinished household work.
- Single parents: limited backup and more consequential scheduling conflicts.
- Co-parents across homes: handoffs, permissions, and clear information boundaries.
- Parents with several children: overlapping activities and different school systems.

Ask about recent behavior before showing the feature list. Enthusiasm alone is not evidence of repeat use or willingness to pay.
